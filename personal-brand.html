The Power of Your
Personal Brand

The essential elements that define your presence
By Jennifer Schuman, Horizon Portraits
← Back to the Magazine

Your personal brand is often your first introduction — long before the handshake, the meeting, or even the conversation.

Whether someone discovers you through LinkedIn, a search, or a referral, the impression they form is shaped by how clearly and consistently you present yourself. Jennifer Schuman argues that personal branding isn't reserved for celebrities or influencers anymore — it's a strategic necessity for anyone who wants to stand out, build trust, and create opportunity. At its core, she writes, it isn't about perfection. It's about alignment: when your visual presence, your messaging, and your reputation all move in the same direction, a brand becomes magnetic.

Where Do You Stand?

To help readers locate themselves, Jennifer poses a quick, honest self-assessment — a handful of questions covering how clearly people understand your work when they meet you, how they respond when you describe what you do, how confidently you can name what sets you apart from your competition, and how visible your brand actually is online. The exercise isn't meant to flatter. It's meant to show you exactly where the gap is between how you see yourself and how the market currently sees you — which is the first real step toward closing it.

Two Portraits, Two Truths

To make her point, Jennifer turns to two of the most photographed women of the last century. In 2007, Annie Leibovitz photographed Queen Elizabeth II in full ceremonial regalia inside Buckingham Palace's White Drawing Room — every historic detail in place, radiating dutiful, steadfast authority. A decade earlier, Vanity Fair sent Mario Testino to photograph Diana, Princess of Wales, and Testino made the opposite choice entirely: a bare former schoolroom with whitewashed brick and cement floors, simple modern poses, none of the ceremonial weight. Diana later called it one of the happiest days of her life.

Jennifer's point isn't that one setting beats the other. It's that both portraits worked for the same reason — each one was honest about exactly who its subject was in that chapter of her life. A historic, powerful setting can be just as meaningful as a stripped-down one, she writes, as long as it's actually true to you. That, more than polish or perfection, is what a personal brand is supposed to capture.

“When image, presence, and purpose align, a personal brand becomes unforgettable.” — Jennifer Schuman

Time to Elevate

The invitation Jennifer leaves readers with is simple: this is the moment to re-think and re-define who you are today, and how to leverage the value you've already built — not by starting over, but by finally letting your presentation catch up to your presence.
